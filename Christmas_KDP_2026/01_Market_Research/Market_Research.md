# Phase 1: Market Research, Christmas Picture Books (for Christmas 2026)

Research date: 4 November 2026. Sources were web searches run on that date (listed at the end).
**No sales figures, BSR ranks or market statistics appear here.** None were found in a verifiable form, so none are claimed.
Before you publish, check the live Amazon Best Sellers lists for *Children's Christmas Books* and *Children's Bedtime & Dreaming Books*. They change daily.

## 1. What the market looks like

**Evergreen titles.** Curated "best Christmas picture book" lists keep returning to the same classics: *How the Grinch Stole Christmas*, *The Polar Express* and *The Snowman*. A self-published title can't compete on recognition. It has to compete on **warmth, giftability, read-aloud quality and visual polish**.

**Recent trade titles (2025 lists).** Recent picks show the themes publishers are investing in:
- **Kindness and joy:** Emma Dodd's *Christmas Joy*, *The Twelve Days of Kindness*.
- **Community coming together:** *Next Stop, Christmas!*
- **Wonder and gift-giving:** *The Snow Globe*.
- **Small-animal heroes:** *The Grumpy Reindeer*.
- **Christmas Eve magic:** *Santa's First Christmas*.
- **Representation:** books where a child sees Santa looking like her own family.

**Common format patterns.**
- Square or landscape trims.
- One large, cute central character on the cover.
- Night-blue and gold palettes with glowing windows.
- Titles shaped "[Name] and the [Magical Thing]".

**Recurring themes:** kindness, friendship, believing, helping Santa, and bedtime / Christmas Eve wind-down endings.

## 2. Review patterns and complaints, and how this book answers them

Press coverage from 2025 (Wired via AI Weekly, Mother Jones, *Canadian School Libraries Journal*, Futurism) documents parent complaints about low-effort and AI-generated picture books:

| Complaint | How *Juniper* answers it |
|---|---|
| Characters change from page to page | Every character is drawn by a single parametric function. Same shapes, colours and clothing on every page (see 03_Character_References). |
| Incoherent plots, no emotional arc | Classic three-act structure, a refrain, setups that pay off (the scarf, "I'm only small"), and a bedtime landing. |
| Illustrations that don't match the text | Every page's art was composed from that page's text. Continuity checks are in the QC report. |
| Unedited errors, distorted anatomy, garbled text in art | Vector art has no AI anatomy glitches. All text is real, typeset text, outside the art. |
| Cover-only illustration, filler pages | 29 unique illustrations, no repeats, and no filler activity pages. |

Amazon requires publishers to **disclose AI-generated content** at upload. We recommend disclosing honestly (see the final report). The NC State study above found most parents were open to AI-assisted images *when screened by people with children's-literature expertise*. That's an argument for ordering a proof copy and having an editor or illustrator review it before launch.

## 3. Five candidate concepts

| # | Concept | Ages | Emotional appeal | Differentiation | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A hibernating hedgehog wakes for her first-ever Christmas and helps a fallen star get home** | 3–7 | Wonder of "first times", bravery while small, giving something precious | An original hook nobody else has: hedgehogs sleep through Christmas. A built-in bedtime ending. Kindness themes, done gently. | Need to avoid generic "fallen star" execution |
| 2 | A mail-sorting robin finds a lost letter to Santa and must deliver it by midnight | 3–6 | Responsibility, a child's wish matters | Letters-to-Santa is a crowded niche | High overlap with existing titles |
| 3 | A snowman who wants to give a present but has nothing | 4–8 | Generosity, self-worth | Strong message | Snowmen-melting endings risk sadness; crowded |
| 4 | A reindeer calf too small to fly who helps by "listening" for lost sleigh bells | 3–6 | Inclusion, everyone has a gift | Reindeer is a top search theme | Echoes a very famous misfit-reindeer story; IP-adjacent |
| 5 | A grandma-and-grandchild advent bedtime story (24 mini-chapters) | 3–7 | Family ritual | Advent format is a strong seasonal use case | Longer book, more cost, 1,000–1,400 words is too short for 24 parts |

## 4. Selected concept: #1, *Juniper and the Little Lost Star*

Why it has commercial potential:
1. **An instantly graspable hook.** "She'd never, ever seen Christmas" makes a first line and a back-cover line that sells the book in one sentence.
2. **Ownable and original.** Hedgehog hibernation is real-world biology turned into a Christmas premise. Searches found no picture book with this premise. They did turn up a well-known chapter-book mouse called "Nutmeg" and an older "mouse and falling star" book, so the hero was changed to a hedgehog called Juniper to stay clear of both.
3. **It fits proven themes** (kindness, friendship, community, Christmas Eve magic) without copying any book's plot or characters.
4. **A bedtime-friendly ending.** It finishes with "Goodnight, little light" and the heroine falling asleep. Parents can reach for it every night in December.
5. **Merch- and series-ready.** A small cast (Juniper, Glimmer, Ruby, Ember, Oakley) and an iconic red striped scarf support sequels (spring, a birthday, "Juniper's First Snow Day") and plush or ornament ideas.
6. **Strong cover read.** A cute hedgehog plus a glowing star on a navy night reads clearly at thumbnail size.
